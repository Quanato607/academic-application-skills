# Outreach Email Patterns

Use this reference to draft concise, advisor-specific outreach.

## Subject Lines

RA:

- RA Internship Inquiry - [candidate area] and [advisor area]
- Prospective RA Intern - [method/domain fit]
- Research Internship Inquiry - [specific lab theme]

PhD:

- Prospective PhD Student - [candidate area] and [advisor area]
- PhD Application Inquiry - [specific shared topic]
- Prospective PhD Applicant Interested in [advisor thread]

MSc pre-entry intern:

- Pre-MSc Research Internship Inquiry - [topic]
- Incoming MSc Applicant Seeking RA Opportunity in [topic]

## Email Structure

Use 180-260 words by default.

Paragraph 1: identity and goal.

> I am [name], a [current role] working on [field]. I am writing to ask whether there may be an opportunity to join your group as [RA/PhD applicant/visiting student].

Paragraph 2: advisor-specific hook.

> I was particularly interested in your recent work on [paper/thread], especially [specific idea]. This connects closely to my interest in [candidate problem].

Paragraph 3: candidate evidence.

> My recent work includes [2-3 strongest evidence points], where I focused on [research taste, not just tools].

Paragraph 4: concrete ask.

> I would be very grateful for the opportunity to discuss whether my background could fit ongoing work in your group. I have attached my CV and would be happy to send any additional materials.

## Style Rules

- Keep the email short and specific.
- Mention one or two advisor works, not a literature review.
- Do not flatter reputation. Discuss research content.
- Use "would be grateful" rather than demanding a meeting.
- Include long-term internship only when the user requests it or when the entry path is RA/intern.

## Domain Lens Examples

Use domain lens only when relevant:

- AI/ML: method, benchmark, training signal, evaluation, deployment.
- Biomedical: disease area, modality, clinical constraint, validation setting.
- Robotics: embodiment, safety, planning, hardware constraints.
- HCI: user population, study design, interaction setting.
- Social science: theory, data, causal identification, field context.
- Humanities: archive, method, corpus, interpretive question.
