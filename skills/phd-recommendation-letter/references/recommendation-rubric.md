# Recommendation Rubric

Use this reference to score and repair recommendation letters for graduate or research applications.

## Admissions Dimensions

Evaluate whether the letter gives evidence for:

- Intellectual ability: reasoning, learning speed, conceptual clarity.
- Research aptitude: problem formulation, experimental judgment, writing, creativity.
- Character and reliability: follow-through, maturity, collaboration, integrity.
- Quality of previous work: role, rigor, independence, output quality.
- Future scholarship potential: readiness for open-ended research and long projects.

## Scorecard

Score each dimension from 1 to 10.

- Recommender credibility: relationship, duration, direct observation, role authority.
- Specificity: concrete episodes, named projects, observable behaviors.
- Research potential: problem taste, failure analysis, independence, trajectory.
- Evidence discipline: claims are supported and properly scoped.
- Authenticity: sounds like a recommender, not a candidate self-statement.
- Bias and fairness: avoids personality stereotypes, vague warmth, and ungrounded character claims.
- Application strength: likely impact on PhD/RA/MSc admission.

Interpretation:

- 9.0-10: Exceptional, decisive letter with direct evidence and strong comparison.
- 8.0-8.9: Strong letter; supports competitive applications but may lack rare comparison or vivid detail.
- 7.0-7.9: Positive but generic; useful but not decisive.
- Below 7.0: Weak for competitive research applications.

## Strong Comparison Policy

Choose comparison strength from the user's requested endorsement level and the evidence ledger. Do not weaken a user-requested strong letter by default, but do flag unsupported strength.

Suggested ladder:

- Natural: "a strong and promising undergraduate researcher."
- Strong: "one of the strongest undergraduate researchers I have supervised or closely collaborated with in recent years."
- Very strong: "among the strongest undergraduate researchers I have worked with in recent years."
- Exceptional: "one of the top students I have supervised in the last X years" or "comparable to a strong beginning PhD student."
- Custom ranking: use the user's exact allowed comparison, including "top 5%", "top 1%", "top 2 among X", or "best undergraduate I have supervised", if the relationship scope and evidence are stated.

When the user requests a high-intensity phrase but evidence is thin, provide:

1. The requested strong sentence.
2. A risk note explaining what evidence it assumes.
3. A safer alternative that preserves strength without unsupported precision.

## Risk Flags

Flag these before finalizing:

- The recommender appears to claim direct supervision over a CV-only item.
- The letter lists venues without explaining what the trajectory proves.
- Publication status is ambiguous: submitted, under review, transferred, accepted, published.
- Praise is generic: diligent, smart, passionate, hard-working, excellent, outstanding.
- The draft repeats the same abstract nouns: judgment, ownership, maturity, potential.
- The tone is too similar to a statement of purpose.
