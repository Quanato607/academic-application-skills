# Authenticity And Bias Audit

Run this audit before finalizing a recommendation letter.

## Authenticity Checklist

The letter should contain:

- A clear basis for evaluation: who supervised whom, when, and how.
- One concrete remembered episode or decision point.
- Specific project contribution with scoped claims.
- A comparative statement that sounds credible.
- One limitation or early-career framing when appropriate.

Signs the letter sounds student-written:

- Too many perfectly polished abstractions.
- The candidate's whole CV appears in order.
- The recommender praises every publication equally.
- The letter overuses "research judgment", "ownership", "maturity", or "potential".
- No sentence sounds like a person who actually interacted with the candidate.

## Bias And Fairness Checks

Avoid:

- Vague warmth without evidence.
- Personality labels that are not tied to research behavior.
- Gendered or culturally loaded praise.
- Diminishing language: "although only", "surprisingly", "for someone like".
- Overemphasis on service, helpfulness, or likeability when research potential is the question.

Prefer behavior-based evidence:

- "He reorganized the analysis by failure setting."
- "She proposed a cleaner evaluation protocol."
- "They challenged whether the baseline comparison was fair."

## Hallucination Checks

Before finalizing, verify:

- Publication venue and year.
- Candidate role and author order.
- Recommender role.
- Project dates.
- Whether the recommender directly observed each claim.
- Whether "accepted", "published", "submitted", or "under review" is accurate.

If not verified, soften:

- "His broader record includes..."
- "According to his CV..."
- "This trajectory is consistent with what I observed..."
